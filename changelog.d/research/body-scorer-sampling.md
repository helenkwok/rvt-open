- **The mesh body scorer tests every vertex of a scored body and scores a large class's 300 bodies by the smallest CRC-32 of their Tag.** `mesh_bodies_vs_ifc.py` tested at most 400 vertices of each body against the other body's surface and scored a class of more than 300 bodies on an even stride by Tag. A body whose untested vertex failed a column passed, the columns moved when a mesh only gained vertices, and one body added to a class moved every chosen body after it in or out. Now every distinct vertex is tested and a class over 300 scores the 300 whose Tag has the smallest CRC-32 (`class_sample`), so one body more or less moves at most one other. Measured on the 27 public models with a Revit export (12 BIM4LCA, 15 Autodesk 2024 samples) on one export of `main`, old scorer against new: 24,439 against 27,178 bodies scored (the capped classes score up to 300 now, not about half), within 1% of Revit's volume 82.5% against 83.2%, within 0.001 ft 72.2% against 72.6%, within 0.01 ft 93.7% against 93.3%, Revit's body within 0.001 ft of ours 85.6% against 85.7%. Seven class rows count fewer bodies (the largest drops: cable carrier fittings 0.01 ft 117 to 93 of 342, flow terminals 290 to 267), the farthest body of 7 models moves by more than 0.001 ft (the office with terrain 0.081 to 0.126 ft), and earlier figures quoted here were measured with the old sampling.
